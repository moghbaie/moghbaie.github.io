About

A whiteboard-first thinker.

I like to start with a marker, not a keyboard. Before I write a line of code I want to sketch the problem, walk through the algorithm by hand, poke at the assumptions, and find the edge cases that would otherwise show up at 2am in production.

My path hasn't been a straight line, and honestly, I think that's my superpower. I started as a telecom engineer at Ericsson, tuning wireless networks across three regional markets. A PhD in systems engineering at Stevens pulled me into complex systems and how people make decisions. Postdoc years at Rockefeller University and ERIBA put me deep inside proteomics and protein interaction networks. Since then I've brought that toolkit to pharma at Johnson & Johnson and AbbVie, and now to hospital operations at Kontakt.io.

The common thread? Graphs, probability, and a stubborn curiosity about how connected things behave.

What I do

Four things I keep coming back to.

01

Graphs & networks

Protein interaction networks, biomedical knowledge graphs, hospital floor plans — if it has nodes and edges, I'm interested. Random walks with restart, network analysis, process mining.

02

Probabilistic modeling

Bayesian methods, mixed-effects models, causal inference and experimental design. My favorite puzzle: reconstructing what actually happened when you only get to see part of it.

03

LLMs that know their stuff

GraphRAG, natural-language search over millions of gene–disease associations, MCP tools and agentic workflows — plus evaluation frameworks that show where models quietly fail.

04

Computational biology

Genomics, proteomics and mass spec at scale — pipelines spanning tens of thousands of samples, and predictions concrete enough that the bench can actually go test them.
